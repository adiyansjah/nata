# x86-64 timing bundle on a settled machine (2026-11-05)

Run `timing-x86-r2` (job `timing`), one `c3d-highcpu-8` Spot VM (AMD EPYC 9B14, 8 vCPU = 4 cores x 2 hardware threads, 16 GB, Debian 12 x86-64, `rustc` 1.99.1), 4,357 seconds of VM time counted, one incarnation, no preemption, 42 steps completed, no resources left after cleanup. Cost: **$0.083** (the run's own report: last machine rate x counted seconds, so VM time only; the 50 GB `pd-balanced` work disk is not in that figure). The first VM create request in the run log failed with a zone stock-out (`ZONE_RESOURCE_POOL_EXHAUSTED`) and a later one succeeded. An earlier x86-64 bundle (`timing-x86`, $0.085, preempted once) started its timed steps at load 2.7-6.0, so its numbers were SANITY ONLY and are not used.

Each timed job step waited for the 1-minute load to fall below 0.5 first (the job's `settle` step; the printed start loads are 0.42-0.49 except for the blocks listed below). The raw outputs are in `bench/results/raw/20261105-timing-x86/` (about 1 MB). The ARM64 counterpart is `20261105-timing-arm64.md`; the two are compared in the last section.

**Gates.** Every correctness gate passed and both `*-exit.txt` files hold `exit status: 0`: hash-ops tests (37 passed, 0 failed), the hash-ops DuckDB differential `check.sh` (7 of 7 queries identical at 1, 4 and 8 threads), `nullcheck` (21 of 21 cases equal the scalar reference), the NULL-path unit tests (26 passed), its `check` (Q1 and Q6: generic-ops, hand-fused and all 7 word-wise steps agree at 1 and 4 threads), its `nullcheck` (26 of 26 cases) and its DuckDB diff (Q1 and Q6 identical), the Arrow pipeline tests (all binaries ok) and its DuckDB diff (12 of 12 configurations identical), the compaction tests (14 passed) and harness self-test (passed), and the push-cost unit tests (16 and 9 passed), smoke pass and harness self-test (passed). The mutation checks were not part of this bundle (the `timing` job skips the hash-ops one, and the NULL-path script printed `mutation check skipped (SKIP_MUTATION set)`); they were run on ARM64 earlier.

**SANITY ONLY blocks (the rule of this record: a printed start load of 0.5 or more, or a SANITY ONLY label, means no conclusion is drawn).** Three blocks are affected; their numbers are printed below and marked, and nothing is concluded from them:

2. **Push cost (section 5).** The harness printed a start load of 0.83 (the build, tests and smoke pass had just run; it waits for 1.0, not 0.5). The harness's own label is `quiet` for every cell, with other processes using 0.00-0.20 cores, but 0.83 is above 0.5.

## 4. Compaction before Arrow wrappers (plan item 18)

270 cells at 10% NULLs, 25 rounds, one thread; printed start load 0.49, rising to 0.57 by the end of that benchmark and 0.60 after the NULL-free control (the harness's own thread adds to the 1-minute average; the harness's SANITY gate, load 2, never fired). The harness self-test passes (injected 1,000 / 5,000 / 20,000 ns measured as 1,032 / 5,066 / 20,078, the identical twin as -1 ns). The quartile columns in `compaction.md` are narrow (the harness does not print a NOISY verdict for this table).

With an INDEX selection (random or four runs), compacting just to run ONE dense function is never cheaper than carrying the selection: 30 of 30 cells, and even at the best point of the grid it is 1.03x to 2.10x slower (1.03-1.21x for plain strings and dictionaries at 8,192 and 65,536 rows, 1.41-1.44x at 1,024 rows, 1.49-2.10x for the numeric types). With a bitmap it depends on the type and the batch size: `Int64` and `Decimal64` never pay at 1,024 rows (best 1.15x and 1.17x) or 8,192 rows (best 1.02x and 1.04x) and pay at every selectivity of the grid (0.10% and above) at 65,536 rows; `Decimal128` pays from about 99% selected at 8,192 rows and about 98% at 65,536 rows (never at 1,024 rows: best 1.02x); plain strings and dictionaries pay from about 99% at 1,024 and 8,192 rows and never at 65,536 rows (best 1.15x and 1.29x). Compacting once to run THREE functions pays at every selectivity of the grid for a bitmap selection at 8,192 and 65,536 rows for all five types. With an index selection at 65,536 rows it pays from about 0.6-0.9% selected for the random selection on `Int64`, `Decimal64`, `Decimal128` and dictionaries; with four runs it pays from about 3.9% for `Decimal128` and 1.5% for dictionaries, never for `Int64` and `Decimal64` (best 1.05x), and from about 95-97% for plain strings. With an index selection at 8,192 rows it pays from about 98-99% for `Int64` and `Decimal64` (the ratio there is 1.05-1.10x from 10% to 90% selected and 0.99x at 100%), about 20% for `Decimal128`, about 50% for dictionaries and about 84-90% for plain strings. A dense pass over ALL rows beats the selection-aware pass above about 63-82% selected for index selections at 1,024 and 8,192 rows (plain strings 63-68%, `Decimal128` 71-72%, `Decimal64` 75-81%, `Int64` 77-81%, dictionaries 77-82%); with a bitmap above about 76-99% (never for `Int64` and `Decimal64` at 1,024 rows). At 65,536 rows it never pays for `Int64` and `Decimal64` (best 1.05x-1.20x), for plain strings and dictionaries only with an index selection (70-71% and 80-81%), and for `Decimal128` above 72-80%. Full table: `compaction/compaction.md` (break-even section) and `.csv`.

## 6. Arrow pipeline (ADR 0001)

Correctness gate first: all tests and the DuckDB diff (12 thread and task configurations, digit for digit) passed. Benchmark, protocol scheduler against the generic-ops engine, 25 rounds, paired ratios (the control column is the protocol arm against itself):

End to end, start load 0.42 (settled): Q1 1.00x at 1 thread, 1.00x at 2, **1.11x [1.08, 1.17] at 4** (control 0.99 [0.94, 1.04]), 1.05x [1.01, 1.07] at 8 (control 1.00 [0.97, 1.04]); Q6 1.00x, 0.99x, **1.16x [1.10, 1.17] at 4** (control 1.00 [0.99, 1.05]), 1.12x [1.08, 1.15] at 8 (control 1.00 [0.95, 1.04]). At 1 and 2 threads the protocol and the generic engine are the same; at 4 threads the protocol arm is 11% and 16% slower end to end, a difference larger than the spread of its control (minimum / median at 4 threads: Q1 protocol 41 / 46 ms against generic 40 / 41 ms, Q6 protocol 23 / 26 against 22 / 22 ms: the protocol arm's median is 3-5 ms above its minimum and the generic engine's 0-1 ms, so the 4-thread cells have slow rounds on the protocol arm and are not clean). At 8 threads the second hardware thread of each core is in use.

Engine only, start load 1.35: **SANITY ONLY, no conclusion drawn.** It printed Q1 1.00x, 1.00x, 1.16x, 1.04x and Q6 1.01x, 1.02x, 1.22x, 1.11x at 1, 2, 4 and 8 threads (`arrow-pipeline-bench.txt`).

Where the two agree. (1) **The cost of strict arithmetic is about zero on the joins and large on grouped aggregates on both**, within -3.8% to +5.1% on `q3`, `q12`, `j3` and +0.9% to +24.7% on the aggregates; the hand-fused loop is within noise on both except `agg_modedate`, which is positive at all four thread counts on both (+5.8% to +8.7% on ARM64, +4.2% to +6.7% on x86-64). (2) DuckDB is faster than our generic engine in most cells on both machines (24 of 28 on x86-64, 23 of 28 on ARM64), by the most on `j3` (1.77-2.00x and 2.06-2.26x). (3) With an index selection compacting to run one function is never cheaper, on both. (4) The Q1 gap to the fused loop is the grouped-sum kernel (61% on x86-64, 56% on N3 and 62% on V2) and the projection pass (21%, 27%, 22%), not dispatch.

Where they differ, plainly. (1) **Which aggregates pay for the checks:** on ARM64 `agg_partkey` and `agg_modedate` pay most (+14% to +19%) and `agg_orderkey` +6-9%; on x86-64 `agg_orderkey` pays most (+9% to +25%) and `agg_partkey` and `agg_modedate` pay +8-16%. The size of the effect is of the same order, but a number measured on one machine does not carry to the other: no single "about X%" holds for both. (2) **Q6:** generic is faster than fused on ARM64 (0.75x, V2 0.72x) and level with it on x86-64 (0.98x); the Q1 gap is smaller on x86-64 (1.59x against 1.79x); in the Q6 profile the decimal filter is first on x86-64 (44.1%) and the date filter first on V2 (40.6%, from the V2 record). ## What this does not show

Anything beyond the seven hash-ops queries, the two Arrow-pipeline queries (Q1, Q6) and the compaction grid, all on TPC-H SF1, on one machine type and one AMD generation; any NULL-path, push-cost or Arrow engine-only conclusion (those blocks are SANITY ONLY above; a re-run with a settle before each phase would be needed); the cause of the `agg_partkey` flat scaling from 4 to 8 threads and of the 4-thread Arrow gap (no hardware counters); the drift between the checks-on and checks-off runs (separate processes); a real object store; the push protocol inside a real engine; the effect of `overflow-checks` on code outside the spikes; a bounded proof that the wrapping sink of step 6 is safe (the argument that it cannot overflow is reasoned, not checked).
