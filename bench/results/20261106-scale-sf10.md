# Scale: SF10 on 32 vCPUs (plan item 32, stage A, 2026-11-06)

Run `scale-sf10-arm64` (job `scale-sf10`, one `n4a-highcpu-32` Spot VM, Neoverse-N3, 32 vCPU / 64 GB, 80 GB data disk, 888 VM-seconds,
$0.097), code `dd60b6c`. Two parts, both against DuckDB on the same Parquet files (TPC-H SF10, 60 million `lineitem` rows, written by
DuckDB's generator). Raw: `bench/results/raw/20261106-scale-sf10-arm64/`.

## 1. Q1 and Q6 thread sweep (the Arrow pipeline spike on the protocol scheduler)

Every block settled first (12 of 12 below 0.5). 5 rounds alternated with DuckDB; wall time of the whole process (Parquet decode included,
and for DuckDB also the CLI's start-up, so the ours / DuckDB column is NOT an engine-only comparison). **All 60 of our outputs equal
DuckDB's digit for digit.**

| query | threads | ours s | DuckDB s | ours / DuckDB | our speed-up | our efficiency | DuckDB speed-up |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| q1 | 1 | 1.249 | 1.588 | 0.79 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| q1 | 8 | 0.170 | 0.272 | 0.62 | 7.35 | 0.92 | 5.84 |
| q1 | 16 | 0.103 | 0.188 | 0.55 | 12.13 | 0.76 | 8.45 |
| q1 | 32 | 0.079 | 0.180 | 0.44 | 15.81 | 0.49 | 8.82 |
| q6 | 1 | 0.636 | 0.763 | 0.83 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| q6 | 8 | 0.094 | 0.173 | 0.54 | 6.77 | 0.85 | 4.41 |
| q6 | 16 | 0.061 | 0.141 | 0.43 | 10.43 | 0.65 | 5.41 |
| q6 | 32 | 0.059 | 0.153 | 0.39 | 10.78 | 0.34 | 4.99 |

(2 and 4 threads in the raw summary: efficiency 0.95 to 0.99.) Reading: Q1 scales to 7.4x at 8 threads (efficiency 0.92) and 15.8x at
32; Q6 flattens past 16 threads, where a run takes about 60 ms and process start, file open and footer read are a large share (not
separated here). The 8-thread result supports the 5x-at-8-threads target of the scan slice's card on this workload, but this is the
spike's pipeline, not the slice's scan.

## 2. The operators-beyond spike at SF10, 32 threads, under `ulimit -n 1024`

`check.sh` with 32 threads, a 1 GB budget and 128 grace partitions: **30 of 30 comparisons with DuckDB passed** (multiset equality, and
row-by-row order on the order-by columns), every spill mode left no spill file. Selected rows (all in the raw `operators-scale-summary.txt`):

| query | mode | rows | seconds | spilled | spill files, most open |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| q18 | in memory, 32 threads | 100 | 13.40 | 0 | - |
| q18 | 1 / 2 / 8 threads | 100 | 35.45 / 26.14 / 15.38 | 0 | - |
| q21 | in memory, 32 threads | 100 | 4.98 | 0 | - |
| join-inner | in memory / grace (1 GB, 128 partitions) | 1 | 4.36 / 0.42 | 0 / 241 MB | 256 files, 256 open |
| sort | in memory (fan-in 4) | 59,986,052 | 16.80 | 3,807 MB | 4 files, 4 open |
| sort | spill, 1 GB budget, fan-in 4 | 59,986,052 | 27.02 | 17,195 MB | 282 files, 13 open |
| sort | spill, 1 task, fan-in 2 | 59,986,052 | 63.92 | 6,698 MB | 5 files, 3 open |
| window-peers | spill, fan-in 3 | 15,000,000 | 4.37 | 1,329 MB | 15 files, 4 open |

Readings. (1) The merge passes keep the open files bounded far below the 1,024 limit (13 open for 282 files written). (2) The grace join is
again about 10x faster than the in-memory join, whose build is single-threaded (`bench/results/20261106-spill-bench.md`). (3) **Q18 does not
scale**: 35 s at 1 thread, 15 s at 8, 13 s at 32; the spike's Q18 plan is dominated by a serial step (not profiled here). The sort and
window run as one ordered stream (ADR 0001 amendment A1, item 3).

## What this does not show

SF100 and larger than memory (stage B, `scale-sf100`); 64 cores (`scale-cores`); x86-64; DuckDB's engine time without its CLI start-up;
why Q18 does not scale (no profile); the real scan of slice 3 (this is the spike's pipeline).

## 3. The same sweep on 64 vCPUs (`scale-cores-arm64`, `n4a-highcpu-64`, $0.132)

Same data (SF10), same script, threads 1 to 64, every block settled (12 of 12), **60 of 60 outputs equal DuckDB's**. Raw:
`bench/results/raw/20261106-scale-cores-arm64/`.

| query | threads | ours s | our speed-up | our efficiency | DuckDB s | DuckDB speed-up |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| q1 | 1 | 1.215 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.534 | 1.00 |
| q1 | 8 | 0.166 | 7.32 | 0.91 | 0.276 | 5.56 |
| q1 | 32 | 0.065 | 18.69 | 0.58 | 0.171 | 8.97 |
| q1 | 48 | 0.063 | 19.29 | 0.40 | 0.178 | 8.62 |
| q1 | 64 | 0.071 | 17.11 | 0.27 | 0.196 | 7.83 |
| q6 | 1 | 0.616 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.743 | 1.00 |
| q6 | 32 | 0.049 | 12.57 | 0.39 | 0.141 | 5.27 |
| q6 | 64 | 0.077 | 8.00 | 0.12 | 0.169 | 4.40 |

Reading: at SF10 neither engine uses 64 cores. Ours peaks at 48 threads for Q1 (19.3x) and at 32 for Q6 (12.6x), then gets slower; a run
is about 50 to 70 ms there, so process start, footer reads and the last morsels dominate. This is a statement about SF10, not about the
scheduler: larger inputs are the SF100 run's job.
