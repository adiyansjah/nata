# ARM64 timing bundle on a settled machine (2026-11-05)

Run `timing-arm64-r2` (job `timing`), one `n4a-highcpu-8` Spot VM (Neoverse-N3, 8 vCPU, Debian 12 arm64), 57 minutes of VM time, **$0.116**. Every timed step waited for the 1-minute load to fall below 0.5 first (the job's `settle` step: a first
bundle had started each timed step at load 2.7-3.3, so its numbers were SANITY ONLY and are not used). The start load of each benchmark is printed in its raw output; **it was below 0.5 only for the hash-ops, perf and compaction benchmarks and for the start of the NULL-path step (0.42-0.49)**. Three blocks do NOT meet the settle rule of 0.5 and are SANITY ONLY by it: the NULL-path phases after the first (printed load 1.4-2.0, mostly the step's own earlier work still in the 1-minute average; section 2), the push-cost benchmark (start load 0.72; the harness itself labels the cells `quiet`, other processes used 0.00 cores; section 5) and the Arrow engine-only block (start load 1.47; the end-to-end block started at 0.49; section 6). This correction was made after the x86-64 write-up found it; the figures are unchanged. The raw outputs are in
`bench/results/raw/20261105-timing-arm64/` (about 1 MB). x86-64 results come from `timing-x86-r2` and are recorded separately.

Steps in the bundle: the hash-ops build, tests, DuckDB diff, nullcheck, memory, and the speed benchmarks with `overflow-checks` on and off; the NULL-path correctness gates and timings; the perf profile on N3; the compaction benchmark; the push-cost benchmark; the
Arrow pipeline's build, tests, DuckDB diff and benchmarks. All correctness gates passed before any timing was taken.

## 2. The word-wise NULL path (plan item 16)

Start load 0.49, but the later phases printed 1.4-2.0 (the step's own earlier work is still in the 1-minute average), so by the 0.5 settle rule everything below is SANITY ONLY; the script's own SANITY ONLY threshold is 2, which is why it printed nothing. Penalty = time with NULLs / time of the same engine without NULLs, Q1, one thread (four threads give the same figures); CPU time, 25 rounds.

| NULLs | old engine | word-wise step 5 | word-wise step 6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1% | 2.30x | 1.06x | 1.06x |
| 10% | 2.15x | 1.15x | 1.13x |
| 50% | 1.54x | 1.28x | 1.24x |

Q6 at 10% NULLs: 2.79x for the old engine, 1.05x for step 5. The word-wise path is also faster than the old engine with NO NULLs (Q1: 44.0 ms against 63.3 ms). **The target of 1.3x is met at 1%, 10% and 50% NULLs on this machine**; the Q1 50% case is close (1.28x).
`overflow-checks` on the checked-sink variants (step 3): 53.4 ms with the checks against 39.6 ms without (+35%); the wrapping-add sink (step 6) differs by about 6%: wrapping is used only for the `i128` sums and `u64` counters, which cannot overflow (fewer than 2^64 addends of at most 2^63 each stay below 2^127; counts are bounded by the batch size). That
argument is reasoned, not checked: a bounded proof of it would make step 6 safe to adopt. Raw: `nullpath/` (density, ladders, stages).

## 3. perf profile on N3 (plan item 19, with the V2 profile in `20261105-perf-v2.md`)

Single thread, batches in memory, settled (load 0.45): Q1 generic 1.34 s against fused 0.75 s for 20 iterations (1.79x, identical to V2), Q6 0.38 s against 0.51 s (0.75x; V2: 0.72x). ## 4. Compaction before Arrow wrappers (plan item 18)

270 cells at 10% NULLs, 25 rounds, load 0.42-0.49; the harness self-test passes exactly (injected 1,000 / 5,000 / 20,000 ns measured as 1,045 / 5,065 / 20,064, the identical twin as 0). With an INDEX selection, compacting just to run ONE dense function is never cheaper than carrying the selection
(1.3-2.0x slower even at 100% selected); with a bitmap it pays at about 92-99% selectivity at small batches and at every selectivity at 65,536 rows. Compacting once to run THREE functions pays from about 1-20% selectivity for numeric types and decimals at 8,192 and 65,536 rows, and never for plain strings with an index
selection. A dense pass over ALL rows beats the selection-aware pass above about 50-60% selectivity for numbers and 65-85% for strings and decimals. Full table: `compaction/compaction.md` (break-even section) and `.csv`.

Readings. (1) **Virtual dispatch costs nothing**: `dyn-direct` equals `static` in every cheap cell (raw tables). (2) **The scheduler and protocol itself costs about 70-130 ns per batch for a one-stage chain, about 410 ns for three stages at 1,024 rows, and disappears into the noise at 8,192 rows and above.**
(3) **Most of the overhead against the control is not protocol cost but the lack of fusion**: the control runs a whole chain in one pass, every protocol arm does one pass per stage (`static` already pays +110% for 3 cheap stages and +410% for 6 at 1,024 rows). (4) The 65,536-row cells with a 3-stage chain on the medium kernel are an outlier (`dyn` +155%): a 512 KB vector is allocated per stage per batch, so the allocator probably shows up as "protocol cost"; not interpreted. (5)
This machine has one hardware thread per core, so the thread counts here are cores.

So the exchange is not free with a small queue and small batches; the cost is of the order of 2-4 microseconds per batch in those cells.

## 6. Arrow pipeline (ADR 0001)

Correctness gate first: all tests and the DuckDB diff (12 thread and task configurations, digit for digit) passed. Benchmark (start load 0.49), protocol scheduler against the generic-ops engine, 25 rounds: end to end Q1 1.01x at 1 thread, 1.02x at 4, 1.06x at 8; Q6 1.01x, 1.06x, 1.14x; engine only Q1 1.01-1.02x at 1-4 threads and 1.14x at 8;
Q6 1.03-1.08x at 1-4 and 1.19x at 8 (those runs take 2-19 ms and sit near the noise). Raw: `arrow-pipeline-bench.txt`.
